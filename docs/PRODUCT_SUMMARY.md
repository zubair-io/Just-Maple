# Just Maple — Product Summary

## The pitch

**Just Maple is where your notes and your conversations finally live in the same place.** Instead of juggling a notes app, a chat app, and a pile of folders that don't talk to each other, Maple organizes everything around **tags** — a single primitive that acts as a folder, a permission boundary, _and_ a chat room all at once. Every workspace is private, offline-capable, and yours to host.

## The problem it solves

Knowledge work is fragmented across tools:

- Notes live in one app, the discussion _about_ those notes lives in another.
- Sharing means wrestling with folder permissions that don't map to how teams actually work.
- Most tools are cloud-locked, so your data isn't really yours.
- Documents are static — they can't capture, compute, or react.

Maple collapses that fragmentation into one coherent space.

## How it works — the user experience

**Tags are the whole model.** Create a tag like _"Project Alpha"_ and you get:

- 📄 **Many notes** — requirements, specs, meeting notes
- 💬 **One chat** — the running conversation for that project
- 🔒 **Permissions** — invite people as owner, admin, member, or viewer

The interface is a familiar two-pane layout: tags and their notes in the sidebar, the active note editor or chat in the main view, with unread indicators showing where activity is happening. Click a tag → you're in its chat. Click a note → you're in the editor.

## What makes it different

**1. Living documents, not static pages.** Notes are rich editors that embed **interactive widgets** — an audio recorder that transcribes on-device, dropdowns and ratings for structured input, AI summaries that read the document's context, live-data displays, and action buttons. A document can _listen_ to its widgets and react: record audio → auto-transcribe → auto-summarize.

**2. Documents that understand their purpose.** Maple infers context from a note's title, its parent tag, or an explicit template — so an _"Interview: Jane Doe"_ note can automatically surface the right recording and scoring widgets.

**3. Truly private and portable.** Passwordless sign-in (passkeys — no passwords to steal), **privacy-first push notifications** that never transmit your content (only IDs — the device fetches the real data), and the freedom to **self-host** on your own server or use the hosted option. Native apps let users pick which server to connect to.

**4. Works offline, everywhere.** Full functionality with no connection — write, edit, organize — and it syncs cleanly when you're back online, resolving conflicts automatically. Available as a web app and native macOS and iOS apps that share the same experience.

## Who it's for

- **Individuals** wanting a private, offline-first "second brain" that mixes notes, tasks, and voice memos.
- **Small teams** that want project discussion and project documents in one permissioned space — without a heavyweight enterprise suite.
- **Privacy-conscious and self-hosting users** who refuse to hand their knowledge base to a cloud vendor.

## Where it's headed

The MVP (notes, tags, chat, offline sync, macOS/iOS apps, voice, AI summaries) is essentially shipped. Next comes **real-time collaboration**, **full-text search**, an **Android app**, and richer widgets (whiteboards, IoT/Home Assistant). The longer-term vision is a **third-party widget SDK and marketplace** — turning Maple from an app into a platform where the community builds new kinds of living documents.

## In one line

> **Just Maple turns folders into living workspaces — notes, chat, tasks, and reactive widgets unified under permissioned tags, private by default and yours to host.**
